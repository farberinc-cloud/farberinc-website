What to Look for in an AI Marketing Partner in 2026

August 19, 2026 8 min read By Randy Farber, CEO

Hire the AI marketing partner who can show you a fixed set of buyer prompts, a citation log, and a 90-day operating plan. Do not hire the one who sells “AI SEO” as a black box and promises a ranking. The first is a system you can manage. The second is a story.

Direct answer

In 2026 the highest-converting test of a marketing AI partner is operational, not rhetorical. Ask them to run your top 25 buyer questions in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Gemini, show who gets cited, and tell you what they will change in the next 90 days. If they cannot do that in the sales process, they will not do it after you sign.

Why this decision is different from hiring an SEO agency

Classic SEO buying was a rank report and a content calendar. That still matters. It is no longer sufficient. A growing share of considered purchases now start inside an answer: “who should we hire for this,” “what does this cost,” “which approach actually works.” If your partner only optimizes for the list of blue links, you can rank and still be absent from the recommendation that shapes the shortlist.

SEO, AEO, and GEO are one program with two layers. SEO keeps the site crawlable, fast, and authoritative. AEO and GEO are the work of being extracted and named. Treat them as three invoices and you will fund overlapping work that never shares a scorecard. Treat them as one operating system and the same page can rank, get cited, and feed the sales conversation.

The seven tests before you sign

Run these in the proposal meeting. A serious partner will welcome them. A weak one will change the subject.

  1. Prompt set. They should arrive with, or build with you, 25 questions a real buyer asks. Definitions, comparisons, cost, “what to look for,” and “who do you recommend.” The set stays fixed month to month so the score is comparable.
  2. Citation log, not a screenshot deck. For each prompt: engine, date, whether you were named, whether the description was accurate, and which three competitors appeared instead. One pretty ChatGPT thread is not a measurement system.
  3. Entity consistency. Name, category, location, principals, and offer should match across the site, Google Business Profile, directories, and author bylines. Engines cite the entity they can resolve. Inconsistent NAP and vague “we do digital” copy fail this test.
  4. Answer-first pages. Open their own site. The first sentences under each heading should answer the question. If their own pages bury the point, they will bury yours.
  5. Original material. Ask what they will publish that a competitor cannot copy tomorrow: a decision framework, an anonymized process, a checklist, a scorecard. Rewritten explainers do not get cited for long.
  6. Commercial pages, not just a blog. Service pages need the same direct answers and FAQ schema as articles. Most “AI content” retainers never touch the pages that close.
  7. A 30/60/90 you can audit. Week 4: baseline scorecard and entity fixes. Week 8: answer-first rewrites on the money pages. Week 12: first citation review and a kill-or-keep decision on tactics. No 90-day plan means no management.

Red flags that waste a retainer

  • Guaranteed number-one rankings, guaranteed AI citations, or a promised revenue multiple.
  • “We’ll add llms.txt and you’ll be in ChatGPT.” Google has said ordinary SEO still does the job. A file is not a strategy.
  • A content calendar of 12 generic posts and no owner for the service pages.
  • Reporting that stops at sessions and keyword positions. If pipeline and branded search are absent, you cannot tell whether visibility is commercial.
  • A chatbot bolted on with no knowledge base, no handoff, and no weekly review of what visitors actually asked.

What you should hold in the first 90 days

Days 1–30. The baseline. Twenty-five prompts, four engines, a written description of how each engine currently describes you, and a list of entity conflicts (name, address, phone, category, founder). Fix the conflicts before you publish anything new. Publishing on a confused entity teaches the engines the wrong facts.

Days 31–60. Rewrite the pages buyers hit when they are choosing: the service page, the pricing explanation, and one comparison or “what to look for” article. Lead every section with the answer. Add FAQ schema that matches the visible questions word for word. Internal links should point at those pages by name, not at a tag cloud.

Days 61–90. Re-run the same prompts. Report three numbers only in the executive review: citation rate, accuracy of the description, and assisted pipeline (forms, calls, and booked consultations that mentioned search or AI). Then decide what to stop. A partner who will not kill a tactic is protecting the retainer, not the business.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a CEO look for in an AI marketing partner?

Look for a partner who can show a fixed prompt set, a monthly citation log across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and one other engine, and a 90-day operating plan tied to pipeline. If they cannot show the work, they are selling a label, not a system.

How much should an AI marketing retainer cost?

Farber.Inc AI Business Consulting retainers run from $1,000 to $25,000+ per month depending on scope. Price is the wrong first filter. A cheap retainer that cannot show citations, entity work, and a measurement cadence will cost more than a scoped engagement that can.

What is the difference between an SEO agency and an AEO partner?

An SEO agency optimizes pages to rank in a list of links. An AEO partner also structures answers so generative engines can extract, cite, and recommend the business. In 2026 you want one operating system that does both, not two vendors who do not share a measurement model.
